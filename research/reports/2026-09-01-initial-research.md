## Initial thesis: Watch / defer

Microsoft merits full valuation work because its fiscal 2026 results show substantial cloud-led growth and a large contracted-revenue base. However, I would **watch/defer rather than initiate** at this stage because no intrinsic-value range, market-price comparison, normalized free-cash-flow forecast, or explicit assessment of AI-infrastructure capital intensity has yet been completed. The current conclusion is an admission decision for research, not an investment recommendation.

## MD&A update - fiscal 2026 operating read-through

Microsoft's fiscal 2026 MD&A describes a company with strong cloud-led revenue growth, but also with a materially higher infrastructure-investment burden. The table below separates reported facts from their preliminary valuation relevance.

| MD&A observation | Reported evidence | Preliminary valuation implication |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated growth | Revenue increased $50.1 billion, or 18%, to $331.8 billion; operating income increased 21% to $155.2 billion. | A valuation should test whether growth converts into sustainable operating-profit and FCFF growth rather than extrapolating a single year. |
| Microsoft Cloud | Microsoft Cloud revenue increased 27% to $214.4 billion. | Cloud is a central revenue-growth driver and should receive explicit forecast assumptions. |
| Intelligent Cloud | Segment revenue increased $31.5 billion, or 30%; Azure and other cloud services grew 41%. | The base case needs an explicit Azure growth glide path; a high starting rate is not a permanent-growth assumption. |
| Contracted revenue | Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) increased 84% to $678 billion. | RPO supports visibility, but timing, conversion, and margin on contracted revenue must be modeled rather than assumed. |
| Cloud-margin pressure | Microsoft Cloud gross-margin percentage decreased to 66%; the MD&A attributes pressure to AI-infrastructure investment, growing AI-product usage, and a sales-mix shift toward Azure. | A revenue-only upside case would be incomplete. The valuation must test gross margin, depreciation, and reinvestment sensitivity. |
| Intelligent Cloud costs | Intelligent Cloud cost of revenue increased 44%, driven by AI-infrastructure investment supporting customer demand; segment operating income still increased 28%. | The key investment question is whether future utilization and efficiency gains can offset the current cost ramp. |
| Productivity and Business Processes | Segment revenue increased 16% and operating income increased 20%; Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud revenue grew 17%. | This provides an additional recurring-commercial growth engine, but should be forecast separately from Azure because the business economics differ. |
| More Personal Computing | Segment revenue decreased 1%, with XBOX content and services revenue down 5%, partly offset by Search advertising growth. | A blended company-level growth rate can obscure slower or declining consumer/hardware businesses. |
| Liquidity and capital deployment | Cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments fell to $76.8 billion from $94.6 billion. The company states it expects to continue investing in datacenters, cloud offerings, and AI infrastructure. | The bridge and DCF should distinguish operating liquidity from excess cash and should model capital intensity rather than treating all cash as automatically distributable. |

### Initial MD&A conclusion

The MD&A strengthens the case for researching Microsoft: Azure growth, Microsoft Cloud growth, and RPO expansion point to significant demand. It also sharpens the central risk to the initial thesis: AI-related infrastructure spending may absorb cash flow and pressure margins faster than revenue growth converts to durable per-share value. The next valuation work should therefore focus on the relationship among Azure growth, cloud gross margin, capital expenditures, depreciation, and free cash flow.

## Enterprise-to-equity bridge convention and known-answer check

The enterprise-to-equity bridge will start with enterprise value, subtract total debt and any other debt-like claims judged appropriate, and add non-operating cash only to the extent it is not required for operations. Equity value will then be divided by diluted shares outstanding to derive a per-share value.

For the synthetic known-answer exercise completed in class, the bridge should reconcile exactly to the instructor-provided per-share answer before applying the convention to Microsoft. No Microsoft per-share value is claimed here because enterprise value and the treatment of excess cash have not yet been estimated.

## Consequential assumptions

1. The FY2026 10-K is the appropriate primary baseline for this analysis as of the valuation date.
2. Debt is initially measured at its June 30, 2026 carrying amount of $40,294 million, before any adjustment for leases, pension items, or other debt-like claims.
3. Cash is initially treated separately from short-term investments; the valuation must determine how much of either balance is operationally required before adding any amount to equity value.
4. The diluted weighted-average share count of 7,453 million is the starting per-share denominator; a later valuation should reconcile it with current shares outstanding and dilution from awards or convertibles.
5. Azure/cloud growth and commercial RPO are useful operating indicators, but neither alone proves durable margin, free-cash-flow, or valuation support.
6. The base case must not assume that current cloud growth or RPO growth persists indefinitely; it must state a period-by-period growth path, margin path, and reinvestment requirement.
7. AI infrastructure investment is treated as economically consequential even if accounting classification, useful life, and cash-flow timing require further reconciliation.

## Unknowns and uncertainties

- The current market price, market capitalization, and implied expectations embedded in that price on September 1, 2026.
- Sustainable Azure growth, cloud gross margin, and the effect of AI product usage and infrastructure spending on future margins.
- Whether the $55,908 million short-term-investment balance is excess cash or required for operations and capital commitments.
- The appropriate treatment of operating leases, other long-term liabilities, investments, and any noncontrolling or debt-like claims in the bridge.
- The appropriate revenue, operating-margin, reinvestment, tax, WACC, and terminal-growth assumptions for an FCFF valuation.
- The extent to which large commercial RPO balances convert to revenue and cash flow at expected margins and timing.
- The level and duration of AI-infrastructure capital expenditures, the resulting depreciation profile, and the utilization needed to support cloud-margin recovery.
- Whether current Microsoft Cloud and Azure growth rates reflect durable demand, temporary capacity catch-up, large-customer concentration, or a mix of these factors.

## Research plan

1. Reconcile the balance-sheet cash, investments, debt, and diluted-share figures to the evidence ledger, including page/table citations and unit checks.
2. Build a historical operating model using revenue, operating income, depreciation, capital expenditures, working-capital movements, and taxes from the 10-K.
3. Analyze Azure/cloud growth, Microsoft Cloud margins, AI-infrastructure capex, and commercial RPO as drivers of forecast revenue, margin, and reinvestment; build a specific bridge from revenue growth to FCFF.
4. Construct an FCFF valuation with explicit base, downside, and upside cases; separately test WACC, terminal growth, revenue growth, operating margin, and reinvestment assumptions.
5. Complete the enterprise-to-equity bridge with documented treatment of cash, investments, debt, leases, and diluted shares.
6. Compare intrinsic-value results with a selected peer set and a reverse-DCF/implied-expectations analysis.
7. Identify valuation reversal conditions, including evidence that cloud growth, margins, capital intensity, or contracted-revenue conversion differ materially from the base case. A key reversal condition is a sustained mismatch between AI-infrastructure spending and the revenue, margin, or cash-flow conversion expected from it.
